UNINVITED 
Reposting Martina Menegon's statement created in response to the Wiener Festwochen festival's invitation of Peter Thiel

As a participating curator and artist of TRANSMYTHICAL (a project by the Transmediale Kunst class, with student interventions, installations, performances, concerts, workshops), I condemn Milo Rau's decision to invite Peter Thiel to the Wiener Festwochen.
Inviting a techno-authoritarian, anti-democratic, misogynist billionaire to an art festival was harmful and disrespectful to the communities the festival claims to serve, to the artists in its own programme, and most directly to my students, whose immense effort and trust were betrayed by this decision [see their statement here: https://tinyurl.com/5ef8adyn #WeRefuseToTurnPowerIntoSpectacle]

[Update: 31st May, 2026]: After a public "debate", Wiener Festwochen Artistic Director Milo Rau withdrew the invitation of Peter Thiel. Yet his statement called the invitation "extremely compelling and thematically consistent" and said he "unfortunately had to decide against the planned event" out of "responsibility for the overall programme". Even in the act of un-inviting, the festival director positions Thiel as someone worth having, who only had to be let go because of external pressure. 
A reluctant concession, not a principled reversal.
Cancelling (under pressure) does not undo the act of centering Peter Thiel, nor the fact that Milo Rau decided Peter Thiel belonged on that stage. The invitation was the choice that mattered, and he should never have been invited in the first place.
#TheInvitationWasTheHarm

Curating is choosing where to direct attention. The festival made its choice. This is mine.
I have changed my original artwork in protest, to include this statement, information on who Thiel is and the harm of platforming, and a list of people whose work builds what he cannot imagine.

Why Platforming Peter Thiel Is Harmful?

Platforming Is Not Neutral.
The invitation alone (even withdrawn) already did the work of making his presence appear legitimate. Research on media platforming shows this clearly.

Bolet and Foos (2025) conducted population-based experiments using real-world interviews with far-right activists on television. Their findings: uncritical platforming increases both personal agreement with extreme statements and the perception that those views are widely held in society. Critically, even when the interviewer robustly challenged the speaker, the platforming still made audiences believe those views were more socially accepted than they actually are. The normalisation effect persisted regardless of critical framing. 
Satta (2021) argues that when a speaker whose ideas should not be legitimated is given an institutional platform, that platform "creates misleading evidence" about the speaker's credibility. Giving a platform conveys legitimacy, and that legitimacy cannot be undone by a critical introduction, or by withdrawing it after the fact. 
Valentim (2024) shows that when people see a controversial figure treated as a legitimate participant in public life, the social norm against those views erodes. 
Heinze and Off (2026) document how mainstream institutions legitimise far-right actors simply by treating them as normal participants in public debate. 

The invitation was the signal. The cancellation does not unsend it. Peter Thiel gets to perform the role he has perfected: the persecuted truth-teller silenced by the intolerant left. 
Once again: Critical Framing Does Not Protect Against Normalisation. A critical frame is still a stage. An invitation that should never have been sent cannot be made right by withdrawing it.

Women, Queer, Indigenous, and BPOC Tech Artists We Could (and Should) Be Centering

What follows is a non-exhaustive list of women, queer, trans, Indigenous, and BPOC tech artists, scholars, and builders of technologies of relation, foregrounding opacity, refusal, Indigenous knowledge, collective care, queer futurity. 
They are the ones already building what Thiel cannot imagine. This is who we should be centering.

Each name in this list is an invitation to learn, to cite, to commission, to centre. 

Caroline Sinders >>> Feminist Data Set. Asks what it would look like to code alternative futures using intersectional feminist data creation methods. A direct methodology with free toolkit and resources.
URL: https://carolinesinders.com/feminist-data-set/

Christie Neptune >>> Light Years Apart. Uses algorithmic resistance tactics within Black artistic interventions into digital surveillance. Interactive mediated performance that reconfigures visibility under surveillance regimes. 
URL: https://backslash.org/art/christie-neptune-light-years-apart

Dictionary of Dark Matters >>> A self-published people's dictionary written over the course of ten weeks during the Summer 2020 session of “Dark Matters: On Blackness, Surveillance and the Whiteness of the Screen.” It is an abundance of poetry, prose, creative writing, personal history and illustration to name and navigate predatory systems.
URL: https://darkmatters.xyz/

Holly Herndon >>> Composer and artist whose AI work (PROTO, Spawn, Thee, Artificial Womb) explores collective voice, machine listening, and ethical AI collaboration. Her practice treats AI as a shared, transparent medium rather than a proprietary tool of control. Sonically and politically, she builds the opposite of the surveillance-and-profit model.
URL: https://herndondryhurst.studio/

Joy Buolamwini >>> Founder of the Algorithmic Justice League. Her Gender Shades study (with Gebru) exposed that commercial facial recognition from Microsoft, IBM, and Amazon misidentifies Black women up to 35% of the time. These is the exact biometric technology Palantir deploys for surveillance. Her work directly undermines the premise of automated policing and border tech.
URL: https://www.poetofcode.com/

Keoni Mahelona >>> Co-founder and Chief Technology Officer of Te Hiku Media. Built Papakupu, an open-source speech recognition engine for te reo Māori, refusing to license the data to Big Tech. His work centers Indigenous data sovereignty, community ownership, language revitalisation through technology that serves the people, not the shareholder.
URL: https://tehiku.nz/

Lauren Lee McCarthy >>> LAUREN. Performs as a human Amazon Alexa, opening the black box of surveillance in the domestic sphere. Questions the power dynamics of inviting AI into intimate spaces.
URL: https://get-lauren.net/LAUREN

Praba Pilar >>> Techno-Tamaladas / Nixtamalízaté-té-té. Proposes "technologies of life" versus "technologies of extraction." Uses nixtamalization (the Indigenous technology of processing corn) as a model for technological futurity beyond colonial relations. Directly counters the extraction logic of Palantir. 
URL: https://www.prabapilar.com/queercornutopia

Qingyi Ren >> Queer feminist technoscience researcher. Their work "exposes how technical systems reproduce violence — and imagines queerer, embodied alternatives." Directly confronts the cisheteronormative infrastructure of machine learning / AI.
URL: https://renqingyi.com/ 

Safiya Umoja Noble >>> Author of Algorithms of Oppression: How Search Engines Reinforce Racism (2018). A foundational text showing how algorithmic systems reproduce structural racism and sexism. 
URL: https://safiyaunoble.com/

Sasha Costanza-Chock >>> Author of Design Justice: Community-Led Practices to Build the Worlds We Need (2020). Queer trans scholar whose work centres the expertise of those most impacted by harmful tech. 
URL: https://www.schock.cc/

Shu Lea Cheang >>> Shu Lea Cheang is a pioneering Taiwanese-American artist and filmmaker widely celebrated for her foundational contributions to net art, cyberfeminism, and queer science fiction. Her work builds queer sci-fi worlds that challenge surveillance society. Long-term anti-surveillance activist.
URL: https://globalvoices.org/2022/06/29/why-gender-dissent-and-queer-sci-fi-can-challenge-surveillance-an-interview-with-artist-shu-lea-cheang/

Tiara Roxanne >>> Scholar and performance artist. Rethinks AI through a decolonial cyberfeminist lens. Work directly addresses the "Kill Cloud", the surveillance infrastructure ICE uses at the US-Mexico border to capture migrants and Indigenous people (built by Palantir).
URL: https://disruption.institute/fellows/tiara-roxanne

Timnit Gebru >>> Eritrean-Ethiopian computer scientist and one of the most visible critics of the exact tech ecosystem Thiel represents. She co-led the Ethical AI team at Google until she was fired in 2020 after refusing to retract a paper documenting the environmental and societal harms of large AI models. She is the founder of the Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR), an institute explicitly built to be independent of Big Tech funding and control.
URL:https://dair-institute.org/ + https://s10251.pcdn.co/pdf/2021-bender-parrots.pdf

Zainab Aliyu >>> Works on the cybernetic entanglements of race, surveillance, and ancestral memory. Community resource countering the colonial and capitalist legacies of biometric surveillance.
URL: https://zai.zone/
